title: "Is 'The Wish' Actually From 1995?"

---

Is it possible that "The Wish" (released on Tracks) actually dates to the 1995 "Somewhere North of Nashville" sessions?

I've long believed that it could've been from the fabled 1986/87 "Nashville" sessions. Where Bruce was recording (or planned to) work with a band of Nashville session players. However, word got out and Bruce pulled the plug. It is unknown what, if anything, was recorded during those sessions. Brucebase lists only a harmonica part which was eventually overdubbed onto "Spare Parts". However, my theory has been that "The Wish" was at least attempted during those sessions.

Although I've been reconsidering that, and I now think the version we have is actually from the 1995 "Daylight"/"Somewhere North of Nashville" sessions.

# The Evidence

What initially had me thinking about this was the presence of the pedal steel and organ. Not the mention Bruce shouting directions ("to the right now").

The listed credits on Brucebase, give this lineup:

- Bruce Springsteen: vocals, guitar, bass, keyboards, tambourine, maracas
- Gary Mallaber: drums

The liner notes only credit Gary on drums, and Bruce is given a blanket credit of vocals, guitar, and harmonica on the whole D3 "unless otherwise noted", which it isn't.

If you listen to the track, you can pretty clearly pick out pedal steel and organ as well as drums. No source I've found even mentions these instruments, let alone who could've played them. Brucebase credits Bruce with keyboards, but then who played the pedal steel?

The song is listed as being recorded February 22, 1987 at Thrill Hill (Bruce's home studio). Likely this would've been just Bruce and (presumably) Toby Scott producing/engineering. If this track was just Bruce, then who is he talking to when saying "to the right now" and "one more time around"? If you go off of the listed credits, Bruce built this track by himself one overdub at a time, similar to his other demo sessions. Possibly like this:

- Track 1: vocal and guitar
- Track 2: organ
- Track 3: bass
- Track 4: pedal steel
- Track 5: backing vocal
- Track 6: keys/synth
- Track 7: drums (added by Gary Mallaber at an unknown date).

*Note: The above is purely speculation, and isn't based on any sort of reality or inside info, just my hypothetical scenario for how he would've recorded this song.*

But, I don't think that this song was just Bruce and Gary Mallaber. It sounds too "together" to have been built track by track. Everything sort of "gels" in a way I can't entirely explain. Plus, you have Bruce calling out directions. I instead submit that this song wasn't a solo endeavor at all, but rather a full band recording. But what band? and when?

I think I know.

# Somewhere North of Nashville Sessions

These sessions took place in May/June 1995. Bruce called together some old friends and some new players for a series of sessions at his Beverly Hills home/studio. These were:

- Garry Tallent: bass
- Danny Federici: keyboards
- Patti Scialfa: backing vocals
- Soozie Tyrell: violin

And among those new included:

- Gary Mallaber: drums
- Marty Rifkin: pedal steel

Gary had played with Van Morrison and Steve Miller, and also played on most of Bruce's "Lucky Town". As for Marty, per the *Tracks II: The Lost Albums* liner notes:

> He wanted a pedal-steel guitar player added to the mix, and Marty Rifkin was drafted, having recently contributed to Tom Petty's solo album Wildflowers. Springsteen himself handled the remaining guitar duties.

Bruce is not known to have ever played pedal steel guitar, always relying on others to play it (Marty Rifkin, Nils Lofgren, etc.) I have found no evidence to suggest that Bruce plays (or even knows how to play) pedal steel guitar. If we go with the accepted lineup, and Bruce played it himself, it would mark the only known occasion of it happening. Also, much like the bass part, it sounds too good for someone who isn't known to play one.

These sessions were originally for a collection of songs which would eventually become *The Ghost of Tom Joad*, but Bruce also had some more upbeat "country" stuff written. This material would be done in the day, with the slower *Joad* material recorded at night. For years, these were known as the "Daylight" sessions, and the material was long speculated but never known for certain until their inclusion on *Somewhere North of Nashville* as part of the Tracks II box set.

Along with the new stuff, these sessions included the return of BITUSA outtakes "Stand on It" and "Janey, Don't You Lose Heart". And, as I believe, "The Wish" was done during these sessions as well.

Think about it. You have a band tackling more upbeat country-esque material (which definitely applies to The Wish). The band includes a top notch session guy on pedal steel, a keys player in Danny, a bassist in Garry Tallent, and (most importantly) ***Gary Mallaber on drums***.

These sessions were loose, featuring tracks cut with the band together rather than built track-by-track via overdubs. A loose session such as this would have the band playing and Bruce calling out directions, cutting a take "live in flight". Other songs on SNN like "Stand On It" feature this as well.

> "STAND ON THAT THING MARTY"

The Wish has directions throughout, and mostly at the end:

> Woo!<br>
> Alright Now! You're lookin good!<br>
> To the right now!<br>
> Yeah, to the left!<br>
> Alright, keep on dancing now! Keep on dancing!<br>
> Aw one more time around now!<br>

Would he be shouting all this if he was the only one in the room? I don't think so.

Additionally, there are a few things in the track I found:

- The bass part is far busier than any of Bruce's known bass parts. Bruce's bass parts on stuff like "Goin Cali'" and "Loose Change" are pretty simple and most just accompaniment, nothing too fancy. Meanwhile the "Wish" bass part is much more active. Which makes me think that it isn't Bruce but instead Garry Tallent (whose parts fit with this more active style.)
- The vocal doesn't sound 100% like 1987 "Tunnel of Love"-era Bruce.  The vocals sound much closer to something like "Repo Man" than they do anything from TOL.

# Conclusion

I believe that version of "The Wish" released on Tracks actually dates from May/June 1995, rather than February 1987 as previously thought. Between the presence of the band, Bruce's vocal, as well as the lineup for both SNN and The Wish sharing a drummer.

The proposed lineup for "The Wish" is as follows:
> Bruce Springsteen: vocals, guitar<br>
> Garry Tallent: bass<br>
> Danny Federici: organ, synthesizer<br>
> Gary Mallaber: drums<br>
> Marty Rifkin: pedal steel guitar<br>

With this, I believe that this take is actually "V2" as listed on Brucebase. With the unknown and uncirculating "V1" being his original demo take from February 1987. It could also be a previously unknown "V3", entirely separate from the 1987 version. This era of Bruce is very much undocumented, so the exact session history for this song may never be publicly known.

All we have to go off of is the music.
